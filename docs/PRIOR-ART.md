# Prior art and claim boundary

SLATE competes on execution, partner depth and honesty. It does not compete on
conceptual novelty, and this document exists so a judge does not have to discover
that for themselves.

## Required wording

> Our documented search found standard pipeline monitoring stacks, automated QC
> and deliverables products, predictive pre-miss alerting in logistics, and
> existing writing on applying error budgets to delivery. We did not confirm
> whether media supply chain orchestrators predict deadline risk, and we do not
> claim they cannot.

## What already exists

| Category | Named products or sources | What they do | How SLATE differs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pipeline observability | Prometheus + Grafana, the standard media-infrastructure stack | Metrics, logs, traces and dashboards over encoding infrastructure | **Commodity, and we are not claiming it.** SLATE uses exactly this stack, through the official Grafana MCP server, as the agent's operational sense rather than as an after-the-fact dashboard |
| Automated QC and deliverable conformance | Telestream Vidchecker, Interra BATON, Shade, EditShare | Per-asset conformance against a delivery specification: video, audio, loudness, captions | Per-asset correctness, evaluated file by file. Neither product answers "will the remaining work fit before the contractual date" |
| Predictive pre-miss alerting | Logistics and supply-chain platforms | Forecast completion against an SLA window from current queue depth, and alert before a breach | **The same mechanic, in another industry.** We name it explicitly rather than presenting the forecast as new |
| Error budgets applied to delivery | SRE practice, and published writing arguing error budgets should replace deadlines as the control variable | Budget thinking as a scheduling control | **The reframe has prior art.** SLATE inverts it, so the deadline stays hard and *schedule* is the burned resource, but the move is not ours |
| Media supply-chain orchestration | SDVI Rally, Dalet Flex, Vidispine, Ateme | Task orchestration, workflow automation, cost tracking across a media supply chain | **Unconfirmed in both directions.** We did not establish whether any of them predicts contractual deadline risk, and we do not claim they cannot |
| Generic AIOps incident copilots | Various | Diagnose infrastructure incidents from telemetry | Infrastructure health, not contractual delivery-date jeopardy with a rendition fan-out as the failure unit |

## What SLATE actually claims

Only this: the schedule-budget pattern applied to a **media deliverable pipeline
with per-spec rendition fan-out**, where the failure unit is the transcode of one
spec and the consequence is a contractual QC rejection, with

- the telemetry generated by really doing the work rather than simulated,
- every consequential decision owned by deterministic code or a person,
- the failure class derived from FFmpeg's own output rather than from the
  scenario that was injected, and
- the partner's MCP server as the only path the agents have to observe or act.

## What we do not claim

- That the monitoring is novel. It is not.
- That the mechanic is novel. Logistics ships it today.
- That the reframe is novel. It has published prior art.
- That no media orchestrator predicts deadline risk. We did not establish that.
- That four faults and five classifier cases constitute a production accuracy
  study. They are engineering evidence at engineering scale.
